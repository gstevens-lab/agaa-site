How Annuities Are Taxed, in Plain English

By Greg Stevens · 4 min read

"Tax-deferred" is one of the first things people hear about annuities. It's a real benefit. It also raises the obvious question: deferred until when? Here's how it works.

While your money grows, you owe nothing

Interest inside an annuity isn't taxed year by year. A bank CD sends you a 1099 every January, even if you never touch the interest. An annuity doesn't. Your money compounds on the full amount, and taxes wait until you take money out.

The one detail that changes everything: where the money came from

IRA or 401(k) money (called "qualified"). If you buy an annuity inside an IRA, or roll a 401(k) into one, you never paid tax on that money. So every dollar you take out is taxed as ordinary income, the same as any IRA withdrawal. The annuity doesn't add any extra tax benefit here. People choose it for the guarantees, not the tax treatment.

Regular savings (called "non-qualified"). If you buy an annuity with money you already paid tax on, like a CD or a savings account, only the growth is taxed. The money you put in comes back to you tax-free.

What comes out first

With a non-qualified annuity, the IRS treats withdrawals as growth first. Say you put in $100,000 and it grows to $130,000. The first $30,000 you take out is taxed as ordinary income. After that, you're getting your own $100,000 back, and that part isn't taxed.

If you turn the annuity into a lifetime income stream, the math changes in your favor. Each payment is split into part growth (taxed) and part return of your own money (not taxed), so the tax is spread out over the years.

The age 59½ rule

Take growth out before age 59½ and the IRS usually adds a 10% penalty on top of the income tax. Since most of the people I work with are near or in retirement, this rarely comes up. It's still worth knowing.

What your family owes

When you pass away, your beneficiaries owe income tax on any growth that hasn't been taxed yet. Unlike stocks or a house, an annuity doesn't get a "step-up" that wipes out the gain. That's one reason an annuity isn't always the best place for money you mainly plan to leave to your kids.

Required minimum distributions

If the annuity is inside an IRA, required minimum distributions still apply, starting at age 73 for most people today. Most annuity companies can calculate and send your RMD for you. A non-qualified annuity has no RMDs.

The short version

  • Growth isn't taxed until you take it out.
  • IRA money: everything you take out is taxable, same as any IRA.
  • Regular savings: only the growth is taxed, and growth comes out first.
  • Lifetime income spreads the tax out over your payments.
  • Heirs pay tax on untaxed growth.
