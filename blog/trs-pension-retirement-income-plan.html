Building a Retirement Income Plan Around Your TRS Pension

By Greg Stevens · 5 min read

Short answer: start with what your TRS pension and Social Security will pay each month, figure out how much is left over, and use your 403(b), 457 and other savings to cover that gap and the things your pension won't: inflation, health insurance before Medicare, and a spouse.

Step 1: Start with your floor

Your TRS pension is income for life, and that's a big advantage most people don't have. Add any Social Security you'll receive. Thanks to the Social Security Fairness Act, that may be more than you expected. Together, that's your floor.

Step 2: Find your gap

Subtract that floor from what you'll spend each month. What's left is your monthly income gap, the amount your savings need to cover. For some teachers it's small, and for others it's bigger than they thought.

Step 3: Plan for what your pension won't cover

  • Inflation. TRS has no automatic cost-of-living increase. Your savings need to grow to keep your buying power up over a long retirement.
  • Health insurance before 65. If you retire before Medicare, budget for coverage, whether through TRS-Care or another plan, until you turn 65.
  • Your spouse. If you take the standard TRS annuity, payments stop when you die. If your spouse depends on that income, compare the survivor options before you retire.

Step 4: Give your savings the right job

Because your pension already gives you lifetime income, your savings usually have a different job than they would for someone without a pension:

  • Keep an emergency cushion you can reach without penalties.
  • Cover the gap between your floor and your essential bills, with steady, predictable income if that gives you peace of mind.
  • Keep part of it growing so you can give yourself the raise TRS won't.

For many teachers, an annuity isn't needed at all, since the pension already does that job. For others, especially those with a big gap or a spouse to protect, guaranteed income for part of their savings makes sense. Either way, it's worth knowing which camp you're in. Step 5: Time your Social Security

If you or your spouse qualify for Social Security, when you claim matters. Waiting raises your monthly benefit for life. With the WEP and GPO gone, it's worth running the numbers again.
